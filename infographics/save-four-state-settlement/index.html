SAVE Source Records: The Settlement and the Court Decision

IOWA · DHS/SAVE · DOJ VOTER DATA · D.D.C. · N.D. FLA.

Two records define Iowa’s place in the national SAVE fight: a four-state settlement that put Iowa inside the DHS/SAVE access architecture, and a federal court decision that vacated the modified SAVE system as unlawful.

The two source records

1. Four-State DHS/SAVE Settlement

CourtU.S. District Court for the Northern District of Florida
CaseState of Florida, et al. v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security
Case number3:24-cv-509-TKW-HTC
FilingSettlement Agreement, ECF No. 30-1
FiledNov. 28, 2025
State plaintiffsFlorida, Ohio, Indiana, Iowa

This settlement is one of the primary records showing Iowa’s entry into the DHS/SAVE side of the federal voter-data architecture. The agreement commits the state plaintiffs and DHS to execute new information-sharing and SAVE agreements, provides for modernization of SAVE, and describes expanded capabilities including bulk upload, Social Security-number search fields, and downloadable verification output.

Precision Note

The agreement says each state plaintiff “may provide” a 1,000-record driver-license sample. This exhibit does not claim Iowa sent that sample. It documents the authority and framework created by the settlement.

2. League of Women Voters v. DHS Memorandum Opinion

CourtU.S. District Court for the District of Columbia
CaseLeague of Women Voters, et al. v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, et al.
Civil action25-3501 (SLS)
FilingMemorandum Opinion, ECF No. 111
JudgeSparkle L. Sooknanan
FiledJune 22, 2026

This decision is the federal court ruling at the center of the current article. The court granted summary judgment to the plaintiffs, denied the federal defendants’ motion and Texas’s motion, and vacated the 2025 modified SAVE system and related DHS and SSA system-of-records notices as unlawful under the Social Security Act, the Privacy Act, and the Administrative Procedure Act.

“This case implicates two fundamental rights that protect Americans from government overreach: the right to privacy and the right to vote.”
The court found that federal agencies “haphazardly combined and repurposed the private information of millions of Americans, including citizenship data that they knew to be unreliable.”

Context Note

The June 22 ruling vacates the 2025 modified SAVE system and the related DHS and SSA system-of-records notices under the Social Security Act, the Privacy Act, and the Administrative Procedure Act, with nationwide effect. This exhibit does not claim the court made an Iowa-specific downstream finding.

Why these records matter together

The settlement shows how Iowa entered the DHS/SAVE access architecture. The court decision shows what happened when the 2025 modified SAVE system was tested against federal privacy, Social Security, and administrative-law protections. Read together, the records explain the core question in the Iowa investigation: what data moved through these systems, what results came back, and where is the audit trail?

Iowa-specific relevance

Iowa Attorney General Brenna Bird and Iowa Secretary of State Paul Pate were named plaintiffs in the four-state DHS/SAVE settlement. Separately, Iowa later transferred voter-registration data to the Justice Department after consultation with the Attorney General’s office. This exhibit keeps the two lanes distinct while giving readers the primary records needed to inspect the source trail directly.

Precision Notes

  • ▸The four-state settlement is separate from Iowa’s later DOJ voter-roll transfer.
  • ▸The settlement says each state plaintiff “may provide” a 1,000-record driver-license sample. This exhibit does not claim Iowa sent that sample.
  • ▸The D.C. court decision vacated the 2025 modified SAVE system and related DHS/SSA notices. This exhibit does not claim the court made an Iowa-specific downstream finding.
  • ▸The exhibit documents the source records. The missing Iowa audit trail remains the reporting question.
