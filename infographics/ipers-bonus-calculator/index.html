      How this is calculated

      Source

      IPERS Investment Team Incentive Compensation Plan for FY2025, produced to Restoring Democracy’s Promise under Iowa Code chapter 22 in response to a request filed June 3, 2026, and reported in The Formula Behind the $25,000: What Iowa’s Next Governor Inherits (Aug. 9, 2026).

      Salary

      $300,802 — the chief investment officer’s 2025 salary in the state payroll record produced by the Iowa Department of Administrative Services.

      Personnel rating

      Footnote 1 of the plan assigns basis-point values to the annual performance evaluation: “Meets Expectations” counts as 15 basis points (0.15%); “Exceeds Expectations” counts as 20 basis points (0.20%). Anything below “meets” disqualifies an employee from incentive compensation outright.

      Market payout ladder

      The plan sets one goal across public and private portfolios alike: 20 basis points of excess return above benchmark. Each basis point of excess return adds five percentage points of payout factor.

      0.01%5%
      0.02%10%
      0.03%15%
      0.04%20%
      0.05%25%
      0.10%50%
      0.15%75%
      0.20%100%

      The ladder stops at 0.20%: anything above 20 basis points pays what 20 basis points pays.

      CIO weights and ceiling

      • Individual performance evaluation: 20%
      • Public market: 40%
      • Private market: 40%
      • Maximum incentive: 50% of base salary
      • Per-person payment ceiling for FY2025: $25,000, assigned by the chief executive through the budget process — not set by statute

      How the components combine

      Each component’s basis points are multiplied by its weight and summed into a blended total, and the published ladder is applied to that blended figure. The exhibit clamps each market component at the plan’s 20-basis-point goal before weighting, because the plan states that anything above 20 basis points pays what 20 basis points pays. Within that constraint, weighting the components and then applying the ladder gives the same result as applying the ladder to each component and then weighting them.

      Important limitation

      IPERS did not produce the CIO’s individual evaluation ratings or award worksheets. This exhibit therefore shows hypothetical outcomes permitted by the published formula; it does not establish how any actual award was calculated.

      Requests for evaluation scorecards and criteria were met with citations to the personnel-records exemption at Iowa Code § 22.7(11).

      Negative market excess

      Negative market-excess treatment is not modeled unless explicitly supported by the published plan. The plan’s payout schedule begins at zero excess and climbs; it does not establish how excess return below the benchmark enters the table. The market controls in this exhibit therefore begin at 0.00%, which means the applicable investment component exactly matches its benchmark.

      Rounding

      Dollar figures are rounded to the nearest dollar for display; the arithmetic is carried at full precision. The 2025 CIO example calculates to $22,560.15 at “Meets Expectations” and $30,080.20 at “Exceeds Expectations” before the ceiling is applied.
