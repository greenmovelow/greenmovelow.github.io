        How this is calculated

        Source

        IPERS Investment Team Incentive Compensation Plan for FY2025, produced to Restoring Democracy’s Promise under Iowa Code chapter 22 in response to a request filed June 3, 2026, and reported in The Formula Behind the $25,000: What Iowa’s Next Governor Inherits (Aug. 9, 2026).

        Salary

        $300,802 — the chief investment officer’s 2025 salary in the state payroll record produced by the Iowa Department of Administrative Services.

        Personnel rating

        Footnote 1 of the plan assigns basis-point values to the annual performance evaluation: “Meets Expectations” counts as 15 basis points (0.15%); “Exceeds Expectations” counts as 20 basis points (0.20%). Anything below “meets” disqualifies an employee from incentive compensation outright.

        The published payout schedule

        The plan sets one goal across public and private portfolios alike: 20 basis points (0.20%) of excess return above benchmark. The plan prints a schedule of twenty rungs against that goal, each 0.01% of total excess worth five percentage points of payout.

        The schedule stops at 0.20%: anything above it pays what 0.20% pays.

        How the components combine

        Each component’s basis points are multiplied by its weight, the three weighted contributions are summed into a single total excess, and the published schedule is read against that total. The plan’s own worked example establishes this order of operations.

        The plan’s worked example

        The FY2025 plan contains a worked example for a Senior RIO with more than three years’ tenure, weighted 20% individual / 60% public / 20% private. After the plan’s tenure calculation the components are 0.15% individual, 0.00% public and 0.12% private.

        • Total excess: 0.054%
        • Award from the payout scale: 25%
        • Annual base salary $170,000 × a 25% maximum salary multiplier = $42,500
        • Calculated bonus: 25% × $42,500 = $10,625
        • Budget limit $25,000; final award $10,625

        That example is what settles how the schedule is read. A total excess of 0.054% pays the 0.05% rung’s 25%, not the 27% a continuously interpolated line would give.

        Between the printed rungs

        Exhibit implementation: between printed payout rungs, the calculator uses the lower published rung, consistent with IPERS’s worked example in which 0.054% maps to the 0.05% / 25% rung. The produced plan does not separately state a general rounding rule, so this is a convention anchored to that example rather than a rule IPERS expressly published. Where it applies, the exhibit says so on the calculation itself.

        CIO weights and ceiling

        • Individual performance evaluation: 20%
        • Public market: 40%
        • Private market: 40%
        • Maximum incentive: 50% of base salary
        • Per-person payment ceiling for FY2025: $25,000, assigned by the chief executive through the budget process — not set by statute

        What the private-market component contains

        Private markets are not private equity. Private equity is one private-market asset class. It sat inside the FY2024 private-market excess calculation and is excluded from the FY2025 one; the FY2025 plan states that “the Private Equity portfolio for IPERS are not included in excess considerations.”

        FY2024

        • Private Equity — 20%
        • Private Credit — 40%
        • Private Real Assets — 40%

        FY2025

        • Private Equity — excluded
        • Private Credit — 50%
        • Private Real Assets — 50%

        The produced records do not establish why the change was made or who authorised it, and this exhibit does not speculate. Private-equity performance reported after the FY2025 incentive period does not establish a reason either.

        Important limitation

        IPERS did not produce the CIO’s individual evaluation ratings or award worksheets. This exhibit therefore shows hypothetical outcomes permitted by the published formula; it does not establish how any actual award was calculated.

        Requests for evaluation scorecards and criteria were met with citations to the personnel-records exemption at Iowa Code § 22.7(11).

        Negative excess and the multi-year layer

        The FY2025 plan’s worked example shows positive and negative annual excess returns feeding a multi-year tenure-weighted calculation — public-market years of +0.10%, −0.05% and −0.10%, and private-market years of +0.30%, −0.10% and +0.20%. Negative annual returns are plainly contemplated by the plan.

        This exhibit does not reconstruct that historical layer. Its public- and private-market controls represent the market-excess component after any applicable multi-year or tenure calculation, which is the figure that enters the weighting.

        What the produced plan does not establish is how a negative total excess would be read against a payout schedule whose lowest printed rung is 0.01%. The controls therefore begin at 0.00% — the point at which the applicable investment component exactly matches its benchmark — and the exhibit does not extrapolate the schedule below it.

        Why only the CIO is a preset

        The FY2025 plan publishes the component weights for each listed row. Several employees share the same job classification while carrying different public/private allocations, so a generic job title does not identify one weighting. Because the produced plan does not give a reader-safe one-to-one mapping from a title to every row, this exhibit uses only the Chief Investment Officer as a named preset. The published rows are listed in the advanced drawer as documentary information.

        Rounding

        Dollar figures are rounded to the nearest dollar for display; the arithmetic is carried at full precision. The 2025 CIO example calculates to $22,560.15 at “Meets Expectations” and $30,080.20 at “Exceeds Expectations” before the ceiling is applied.
