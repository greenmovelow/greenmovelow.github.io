Section 1 • The Record

The Payroll Receipt

DAS produced a five-page payroll-data response for the named IPERS officials in RDP’s open-records request — one page per year from 2022 through the partial 2026 payroll. An “Exceptional Job Performance Pay” column appears in the response for 2023, 2024, and 2025, each time showing $25,000 for the Chief Investment Officer.

  1. 2022 — No column shown No “Exceptional Job Performance Pay” entry appears for the CIO.
  2. 2023 $25,000 Exceptional Pay Labeled “Exceptional Job Performance Pay” for the CIO.
  3. 2024 $25,000 Exceptional Pay Same label, same amount for the CIO.
  4. 2025 $25,000 Exceptional Pay Same label, same amount for the CIO.
  5. 2026 Partial Through 5/08/26 pp Partial-year page; covers the payroll period through May 8, 2026.

The same label, the same $25,000, three years running — and then a partial-year page where the column does not appear.

Payroll-data response produced by Iowa DAS under Iowa open-records law (compensation tables, 2022–partial 2026).

Section 2 • The Boundary

What the Record Shows

The payroll record confirms the payments. It does not explain the rationale.

Confirmed by records

  • The payments were real.
  • They were labeled “Exceptional Job Performance Pay.”
  • The amount was $25,000 in each listed year.
  • The label appears for the CIO in 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Still unanswered

  • Who approved the award?
  • What scorecard was used?
  • Was the benchmark independently validated?
  • Why was the public board record silent?

The records confirm what was paid and what it was called. They do not explain how the award was decided — the public still has not been shown the scorecard.

DAS produced the payroll response, but the response did not include the approval rationale, scorecard, benchmark analysis, or performance criteria.

The payroll record confirms the payments. It does not explain the rationale. RDP sought it — interview questions to relevant officials went unanswered, and DAS priced broader research and compilation at substantial fees. As of publication, no one has provided the public explanation for why these payments were awarded. The question remains: who approved the award, under what criteria, and against what benchmark?

Iowa DAS payroll response. The public record does not explain the approval rationale, scorecard, or criteria behind the payments; no public scorecard for the award was located.

Section 5 • The Stakes

Why This Matters

This is not about whether one executive deserved compensation. It is about whether a public pension system can explain how performance pay is awarded when the benchmark, risk oversight, and approval trail are not fully visible to the public.

Section 7 • Method

Method & Redaction Note

This exhibit is based on payroll data produced by the Iowa Department of Administrative Services (DAS) in response to RDP’s open-records request. RDP is publishing the redacted payroll response so readers can review the source record. Only internal employee identification numbers were redacted. No substantive compensation fields were redacted. RDP does not publish internal reporting notes, source-protection materials, or investigative work product.

Payroll / compensation tables produced by Iowa DAS under Iowa open-records law. Figures and labels are reproduced as they appear in the released record. The payroll response confirms three $25,000 “Exceptional Job Performance Pay” entries for the CIO in 2023, 2024, and 2025; it does not explain the approval rationale, scorecard, or criteria behind those payments.
