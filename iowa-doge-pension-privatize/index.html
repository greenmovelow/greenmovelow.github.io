A CONFLICT OF INTEREST AT THE CORE

The investigation reveals a state task force member is a top executive at a financial firm that stands to gain millions from the very pension changes her committee recommended.

THE TASK FORCE MEMBER

Kathy Kay, Executive VP at Principal Financial Group, was appointed by the Governor to the Iowa DOGE Task Force.

Her Role: To advise on "government efficiency."

→

THE RECOMMENDATION

The DOGE Task Force recommended eliminating the state's pension plan (IPERS) for new hires and replacing it with a 401(k)-style plan.

The Product: Principal Financial Group is a major seller of 401(k) plans.

This section introduces the central conflict. A state official is in a position to create a captive, state-mandated market for the financial products her company sells. The investigation found no public record of this conflict being disclosed or managed.

A NATIONAL BLUEPRINT, EXECUTED LOCALLY

The Iowa proposal wasn't created in a vacuum. A computational analysis reveals it's a structural copy of model legislation from a national conservative group, ALEC.

POLICY DNA: IOWA VS. ALEC MODEL

Structural Similarity 51.4%

The core policy architecture—the "what" and "how" of the reform—is a near-perfect match to the ALEC template.

Textual (Word-for-Word) Similarity 4.0%

The language used was different, providing a veneer of originality and "plausible deniability."

This comparison is the key to understanding the strategy. The Iowa plan wasn't just *influenced* by ALEC; it was an *implementation* of their playbook. This "policy laundering" allows a national agenda to be presented as a homegrown Iowa solution.

KEY PLAYERS & ACCOUNTABILITY

The task force was overwhelmingly composed of business interests, with no representation for public employees. This led to predictable outcomes and a subsequent crisis of accountability.

This chart shows the professional background of the task force members. The imbalance highlights how the interests of 400,000+ Iowans in the IPERS system were not represented in the room where its future was being decided.

PROFILES IN THE INVESTIGATION

Governor Kim Reynolds

Appointed the business-heavy task force and gave it its mandate. Has remained silent on the controversy, despite being the architect of the process.

Kathy Kay (Principal Financial)

The conflicted task force member. Her presence links the policy recommendation directly to a corporate entity that stands to profit.

Reynolds Cramer (Fareway CEO)

Chaired the subcommittee credited with the IPERS proposal. After public backlash, he denied involvement in an email, stating, "I am not involved with the committee making recommendations on IPERS." This created an accountability crisis.
