Who Funds America’s Identity Infrastructure?

Not through annual congressional appropriations. Through immigrant application fees — and billions in federal contracts.

Most federal programs are funded through annual congressional budgets.
This one isn’t.
That changes how oversight works.

The U.S. government’s identity verification systems are financed through fee-based funding mechanisms and large-scale private vendor contracts. This exhibit shows how those funds flow — from immigration application fees to the contractors building and operating the system.

Key Takeaways

The visible layer is the smallest. Palantir's ImmigrationOS ($30M) is a dashboard. IBM's FALCON ($279M) is the engine. The full stack exceeds $4.89B.

Core operational logic is implemented within contractor-maintained systems; public visibility may be limited by redaction and incomplete public SOW fields.

Fee-funded, not appropriated. USCIS operations are largely fee-funded through the IEFA rather than annual appropriations.

The Dashboard

Palantir — $30M

ImmigrationOS

Awarded: April 2025

Function: Analytics dashboard and case-management interface. Fuses data from IRS, SSA, and local law enforcement for deportation pipeline visibility.

The tell: This is the user interface, not the engine. Headlines focus here because it carries a famous name. But the decisions are made below.

Represents just 0.6% of the total contract stack.

The Engine

IBM — $279M

FALCON Task Order

Awarded: May 2024

Function: Builds and operates the Verification Information System (VIS) — the transaction engine behind E-Verify and SAVE. Creates the Person Centric Entity Resolution (PCER) microservice.

The tell: PCER constructs persistent identity profiles — your Golden Record. This is where "verification" becomes "identity modeling."

9.3× larger than the Palantir contract. This is the verification backbone.

The Memory

Peraton — $95M

HART Biometric System

Operator: Peraton (via Northrop Grumman IT acquisition)

Function: Cloud-native biometric database on AWS GovCloud. Replaces legacy IDENT. Stores face, iris, fingerprint data — and maps "Relationship Patterns" between individuals.

The tell: Once a verification system has memory, it stops being a lookup tool. It becomes a national gate.

Hosted on Amazon AWS GovCloud. Key biometric algorithms may be shielded from public records requests by vendor IP protections.

The Full Stack

Total: $4.89B+

Combined Contract Mass

Includes: FALCON (IBM), HART (Peraton), ImmigrationOS (Palantir), plus NEC facial recognition algorithms, AWS hosting, and additional Palantir Pentagon & Navy contracts.

Funded by: IEFA (Immigration Examinations Fee Account) — immigrant application fees, not congressional appropriations.

The tell: The sovereignty of American identity verification has been privatized. The core logic is owned by contractors and shielded by intellectual property law.

This contract stack was executed through procurement actions rather than a standalone authorizing vote on this integrated configuration.

Exhibit II

Follow the Money

Immigrant fees fund the system that verifies everyone. No congressional appropriation required.

Applicant Fees

Visa, green card, citizenship, and work-permit application fees paid by immigrants.

IEFA

Immigration Examinations Fee Account. Self-funding mechanism — bypasses normal appropriations.

USCIS / DHS

IT modernization & operations accounts. Funds allocated internally to verification programs.

Contract Vehicles

EAGLE II, Alliant 2, IDIQs — task orders define scope and deliverables.

Private Vendors

IBM, Peraton, Palantir, NEC, Amazon. Own the logic, the biometrics, and the algorithms.

Immigrants fund the system → the system verifies everyone → private vendors own the logic → you cannot audit the result.
