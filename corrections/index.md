# Corrections & Updates

How we fix mistakes, update reporting, and revise data — transparently

Effective Date: February 10, 2026  ·  Last Updated: October 28, 2026 (corrections log)

No Stealth Edits. No Quiet Rewrites.

If we publish something wrong, we correct it — plainly, visibly, and with a timestamp. If we learn something new, we update the story and explain what changed. In a world built on manufactured confusion, transparency isn’t a vibe. It’s a requirement.

## 1\. Purpose

Restoring Democracy's Promise (RDP) exists to publish accountability journalism that can withstand scrutiny. That means we treat errors as a credibility emergency — not a public-relations problem. When we are wrong, we correct the record as soon as possible and as clearly as necessary.

## 2\. Corrections vs. Clarifications vs. Updates

We use plain labels. We do not hide behind euphemisms.

### CORRECTION (factual error)

A correction is used when we publish a factual mistake (for example, a wrong name, date, quote, number, location, or mischaracterization of a document). Corrections are labeled as **Correction** and include what was wrong and what is correct.

### CLARIFICATION (meaning was unclear)

A clarification is used when the original wording could reasonably be misunderstood, even if strictly factual. Clarifications explain the intended meaning without changing the underlying facts.

### UPDATE (new reporting)

An update is used when new information becomes available after publication (for example, a new filing, a new response from an agency, or a material development). Updates add context or new facts and include a timestamp.

### Small fixes (typos, formatting)

Minor edits that do not change meaning (such as spelling, grammar, or formatting) may be fixed without a published note. If a change affects meaning, it gets an editor’s note.

## 3\. How to Report an Error

If you believe we made an error, please contact us and include enough detail to verify the issue quickly.

-   **Subject line:** “Correction Request”
-   **Include:** the URL, the specific passage, what you believe is wrong, and supporting evidence (documents, links, screenshots, or citations)
-   **Optional:** your name and how to reach you (anonymous requests are accepted, but harder to verify)

## 4\. What Happens After You Contact Us

-   **Acknowledgment:** We aim to acknowledge correction requests promptly (typically within a few business days).
-   **Verification:** We review the claim against primary documentation wherever possible.
-   **Action:** If an error is confirmed, we correct it with a clear note and timestamp. If we disagree, we will explain why.
-   **Accountability:** When appropriate, we describe how the error occurred and what we changed to prevent repeats.

We apply the same standard to text, images, charts, timelines, datasets, code, and interactive exhibits.

## 5\. Where Corrections Appear

-   **On the article:** Corrections and substantial updates appear on the story page, typically near the top or bottom (or both), labeled clearly.
-   **On related assets:** If the error affects a chart, dataset, infographic, or embedded exhibit, we update that asset and note the revision.
-   **In newsletters:** If a significant error was distributed via email, we may publish a correction in a subsequent newsletter issue.

## 6\. Data, Charts, Graphics, and Code

RDP publishes “systems journalism,” which often includes structured datasets, document collections, and engineered visualizations. We treat those as publishable claims — and therefore correctable.

-   **Data revisions:** If a dataset changes materially, we will describe what changed (sources, filters, deduplication, definitions, fields, or methodology).
-   **Versioning mindset:** Where feasible, we keep a dated revision history so readers can understand the evolution of the work.
-   **Methodology notes:** If a correction changes a conclusion, we will say so directly.

## 7\. Social Media & Syndication

If we share a story on social media and later issue a significant correction, we may:

-   post a follow-up noting the correction,
-   update the original post where the platform allows, and/or
-   link directly to the corrected article.

If an error appears in republication or quoting by others, we may contact the downstream publisher where feasible.

## 8\. Unpublishing / Takedown Requests

We do not remove published work simply because someone dislikes accurate reporting. We consider unpublishing only in rare circumstances, such as:

-   credible risk of imminent physical harm,
-   court orders we cannot legally challenge,
-   demonstrably false material that cannot be responsibly corrected, or
-   serious legal/ethical failures that require retraction.

If unpublishing occurs, we aim to leave a transparent record (for example, a retraction notice explaining what happened and why).

Log updated: October 28, 2026. This log lists corrections confirmed from published correction notices and corrected exhibits. It is not yet a complete inventory of earlier corrections.

-   **Oct. 27, 2026 — Correction, clarification and update.** [“Bird Thanked Iowa; The Money Came From Washington,” published June 7, 2026](https://investigations.restoring-democracy.org/p/bird-thanked-iowa-the-money-came), and its companion exhibit, [Bird–RAGA Money Trail](https://restoring-democracy.org/infographics/bird-raga-money-trail/).
    
    _Correction._ The article’s Figure 3, a related passage and the exhibit’s contribution-size chart described counts of entries on the campaign’s Schedule A as counts of donors. The counts were transactions, not unique contributors. The 28 contributions of $5,000 or more came from 27 distinct contributor records. The 59 contributions under $500 came from 47 distinct contributor records; a 60th entry under $500 was a $45 refund, not a contribution. The exhibit also gave the combined share of the three brackets under $500 as 1.5%, the sum of separately rounded figures; calculated from the underlying amounts, it is 1.4%.
    
    _Clarification._ The $350,913.99 that did not come from the RAGA Action Fund included $4,933.96 in bank interest and a refund. These were reported cash receipts on Bird for Iowa’s Schedule A, not donor contributions. The exhibit described $697,000 as expenses shared by the Rule of Law Defense Fund and RAGA; the Fund’s 2024 IRS Form 990 reports it as an expense reimbursement to RAGA, separate from a $1.2 million political contribution.
    
    _Update._ The exhibit listed the RAGA Action Fund’s May 13 and May 26, 2026 contributions to Bird for Iowa as awaiting federal confirmation. Both appear in the fund’s July 15, 2026 report to the Federal Election Commission (filing 1994655).
    
    _Status._ The exhibit was corrected Oct. 27, 2026. The article was corrected Oct. 28, 2026, with a dated note beneath its Oct. 26 note.
    
    The central findings are unchanged: the RAGA Action Fund supplied 80.24% of Bird for Iowa’s reported pre-primary Schedule A cash and $3.525 million in contributions through May 26, 2026. Contributions of $5,000 or more supplied 74.4% of the non-RAGA remainder, and contributions under $500 supplied less than 2%.
    
-   **Oct. 26, 2026 — Correction and update.** [“Bird Thanked Iowa; The Money Came From Washington,” published June 7, 2026](https://investigations.restoring-democracy.org/p/bird-thanked-iowa-the-money-came).
    
    The correction notice at the top of the article, dated Oct. 26, 2026, states that the article described $2,935 as the RAGA Action Fund’s first-quarter 2026 receipts and said the fund’s May 2026 contributions to Bird for Iowa were drawn on balances it already held. Both statements were wrong when published. The $2,935 was the fund’s cash on hand; it reported no receipts that quarter.
    
    The fund’s next federal report, filed July 15, 2026 (FEC filing 1994655), shows transfers from RAGA of $1,425,000 on May 12 and $1,100,000 on May 26 — the same amounts and federal transaction dates as the fund’s contributions to Bird for Iowa in that report. The report does not identify the donors behind RAGA’s transfers.
    
    The article also corrected its description of the Action Fund’s 2025 transfers (removing “operating pool” and “Super PAC layer”), clarified that RAGA and the Rule of Law Defense Fund share an address and officers rather than staff, and corrected four smaller points listed in the article’s editor’s note. These edits were completed Oct. 28, 2026.
    
    The central findings are unchanged: the RAGA Action Fund supplied 80.24% of Bird for Iowa’s reported pre-primary Schedule A cash and $3.525 million in contributions through May 26, 2026.
    
-   **Sept. 4, 2026 — Correction.** [RDP’s SAVE investigation, published Dec. 10, 2025](https://investigations.restoring-democracy.org/p/save-the-benefits-database-now-monitoring). The correction notice at the top of the article states that an earlier version incorrectly described Executive Order 15 as creating a driver-data pipeline linking the Iowa Department of Transportation, the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services and SAVE; identified HHS as the state’s SAVE clearinghouse (subsequent records show the professional-licensing SAVE clearinghouse was established at the Iowa Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing); stated that every Iowa driver was being run through SAVE; and overstated the November 2025 federal settlement as establishing continuous federal access to Iowa’s entire DMV database.
