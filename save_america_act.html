The Identity Backbone

The SAVE America Act doesn't create a new system. It codifies the one already running — and turns voter verification into the mandate for a national identity infrastructure.

Exhibit I

From Voter Check to Identity Pipeline

The bill mandates that states route entire voter rolls through federal immigration databases — and deepens the data plumbing between election offices, DMVs, and DHS.

Data Flow Under H.R. 1238

Step 1 — Upload

State Voter Rolls

Full voter files — names, DOBs, addresses, DL numbers — uploaded in bulk to the federal system.

Step 2 — Match

SAVE / PCER Engine

Federal matching engine cross-references against SSA Numident data, DHS records, and DMV files via linking identifiers.

Step 3 — Flag

Anomaly Flags

Mismatches generate status codes. States receive flagged records — with no requirement for human review before action.

Step 4 — Purge

Roll Removal

States are pressured to act on flags within tight windows. Burden of proof shifts to the voter — to clear a record they may not know exists.

Exhibit II

When the System Was Tested, It Failed

Three states tried to use SAVE or similar federal systems for voter screening. Each exposed failures the bill does not resolve.

Iowa 2024 — DMV-to-Voter-Roll Cross-Match
CRITICAL FAILURE
~88%

of flagged voters were U.S. citizens

The secretary of state flagged 2,176 registered voters as potential noncitizens using outdated DMV records. Civil-rights attorneys discovered that at least 1,899 of those flagged were citizens — many of them naturalized Americans whose earlier immigration status had never been updated in legacy state files.

U.S. Citizens Wrongly Flagged ≥1,899 of 2,176
Actual Noncitizens Found 277 of 2,176

SYSTEM FAILURE MODE

The system is most confident where it is most wrong — naturalized citizens whose records span two immigration statuses.

Texas Pilot — SAVE Bulk Upload Test
STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY
18%

processing failure rate

Texas ran 1,657 voter records through the modernized SAVE system. Roughly 300 queries failed outright — not due to fraud, but because of duplicate entries, formatting mismatches, and data-quality problems. Nearly one in five records could not be resolved.

Queries That Failed ~300 of 1,657

ENGINEERING ASSESSMENT

SAVE was designed for individual benefits queries. At bulk election scale, the failure rate is not statistical noise — it is structural instability in a system never engineered for this caseload.

South Carolina 2023 — Planning Documents
WARNED — NEVER SCALED

Officials warned in internal planning documents that SAVE data would miss naturalization updates, produce name mismatches, and require extensive manual follow-up. The state would need to build an entire parallel review process to account for the system's limitations — undermining the premise of automated efficiency.

OUTCOME: The very system the SAVE America Act would mandate was flagged as unreliable by the states that tested it.

The Pattern

Every state that stress-tested this system found the same result: it flags citizens as suspects, fails at scale, and produces errors faster than any correction process can resolve them.

The SAVE America Act does not address these documented failure modes. It mandates them nationally.

Scope: Beyond citizenship verification

Why This Matters

Voting systems do not need to see your face to confirm citizenship. But once facial images and DMV data are linked to voter rolls through these keys, we have left election administration and entered persistent identity tracking.

As documented in Part V and Part IV of our investigation, this architecture already exists through MOUs between DHS, SSA, and several states. The SAVE America Act would codify it into statute.

Exhibit IV

Three Engineering Questions the Bill Doesn't Answer

When a system moves from low-volume benefit checks to national election infrastructure, engineering questions become constitutional questions.

01

What is the acceptable processing failure rate for a legally mandated system?

Texas's pilot showed an 18% failure rate. The bill contains no defined threshold. When the system fails to return an answer, who absorbs the consequence — the voter, or the state?

Undefined in legislation
02

How quickly does naturalization status propagate across federal databases?

Iowa's false-positive rate was driven by naturalized citizens whose updated status hadn't reached legacy state files. Federal lag can extend weeks to months. Elections do not pause for database synchronization.

Known lag — no remedy specified
03

What is the formal remedy when the federal database is wrong?

The confidential MOUs already offered to states require roll removals within 45 days of a federal flag. There is no independent verification step before removal. The burden shifts to the voter to prove the algorithm wrong.

No defined correction process

This Analysis Is

A documented engineering claim: the system has known failure modes the legislation does not resolve.

A data-architecture observation: identity fields being mandated exceed what citizenship checks require.

A systems question: infrastructure outlasts the political moment that builds it.

This Analysis Is Not

An argument that citizenship should not be verified in elections.

A claim about the intentions of any particular official or party.

A prediction about what this infrastructure will be used for — only an observation that the capability will exist.

The Structural Shift

Election laws change.
Data architecture endures.

When voter verification requires interoperable identifiers, cross-agency matching, and photo-linked credentials, the scope has expanded beyond election law. It becomes a question of how the state constructs, links, and retains identity records — permanently.
