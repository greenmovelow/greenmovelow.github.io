Responsible Disclosure

Security & Vulnerability Disclosure Policy

Coordinated disclosure for security researchers acting in good faith.

If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in our systems, we want to hear from you. This policy explains how to report it, what is in scope, and the protections we extend to researchers who follow it.

Our Commitment

Restoring Democracy's Promise depends on the integrity of its systems and the trust of its readers and sources. We welcome good-faith security research and treat every credible report seriously. This page explains how to report a security vulnerability in our systems, what you can expect from us in return, and the protections we extend to researchers who act in good faith.

This channel is for security vulnerabilities only. If you have confidential information or documents for an investigation, do not use this page. Please use our Secure Tips channel, which is designed to protect sources.

How to Report

Send security reports by email to security@restoring-democracy.org. The authoritative, machine-readable list of contacts is published in our security.txt.

To help us validate and resolve the issue quickly, please include where you can:

  • The affected URL, page, or component.
  • A clear description of the issue and its potential impact.
  • Step-by-step instructions to reproduce it.
  • A proof of concept, screenshots, or relevant request/response details.
  • How you would like to be credited, or whether you prefer to remain anonymous.

Please report promptly after discovery and give us a reasonable opportunity to respond before any public disclosure. If your report contains sensitive details and you require encryption, say so in a first contact and we will arrange a secure channel.

Scope

This policy covers the systems we operate directly. Services we rely on but do not control are handled by their own providers.

In Scope
  • The public website at restoring-democracy.org, including its exhibits and infographics.
  • Our published static assets and configuration served from that domain.
Out of Scope
  • investigations.restoring-democracy.org — our newsletter and articles are hosted by Substack; report issues there to Substack.
  • Third-party platforms we rely on — including Cloudflare, Netlify, Substack, Zoho Mail, Tally, and GitHub. Report vulnerabilities in those services to the respective provider.

Safe Harbor

We will not pursue or support legal action against researchers who, in good faith, discover and report vulnerabilities in accordance with this policy. We consider security research conducted consistent with these guidelines to be authorized, and we will work with you to understand and resolve the issue promptly.

If a third party initiates legal action against you for activity carried out in good-faith compliance with this policy, we will make it known that your conduct was authorized. If you are ever unsure whether a specific action is permitted, ask us first.

Guidelines for Researchers

Good-faith research protects our readers and sources as much as our systems. We ask that you follow these rules of engagement.

Do no harm

Avoid privacy violations, data loss, and service disruption. Access only data that belongs to you or to test accounts you control — never access, modify, or destroy anyone else's data.

No disruptive testing

Do not run denial-of-service tests, high-volume automated scanning, brute-force attacks, spam, social engineering of our staff or readers, or any attempt at physical access to our infrastructure.

Coordinate disclosure

Give us a reasonable opportunity to remediate before disclosing publicly, coordinate timing with us, and comply with all applicable laws throughout your research.

What to Expect From Us

We are an independent newsroom, not a large security team — but we take disclosure seriously and will engage with you directly.

  • We will acknowledge credible reports as promptly as we can, typically within a few business days.
  • We will keep you reasonably informed as we validate the issue and work toward a fix.
  • With your permission, we are glad to credit your contribution publicly; if you prefer to stay anonymous, we will respect that.
  • We do not operate a paid bug-bounty program, but we genuinely value and acknowledge responsible disclosure.

Reports That Usually Do Not Qualify

We operate a static, document-first website. The following generally are not treated as actionable on their own, absent a demonstrated, exploitable impact:

  • Findings from automated scanners without a working proof of concept.
  • Missing or misconfigured security headers, or other best-practice recommendations, without a concrete exploit.
  • Rate-limiting or brute-force observations without demonstrated impact.
  • Clickjacking on pages without sensitive actions; self-XSS; or issues requiring unlikely user interaction or end-of-life browsers.
  • Social engineering, physical attacks, and denial-of-service.
  • Vulnerabilities in third-party platforms (see Scope).

If you are unsure whether something qualifies, report it anyway and let us assess it. We would rather hear about a borderline issue than miss a real one.

Source Protection

We are a newsroom. If, in the course of your research, you encounter anything that appears to be confidential source material, unpublished reporting, or personal data, stop immediately. Do not access, copy, or retain it, and notify us at once. Protecting sources is paramount, and we will treat that discretion as a core part of good-faith conduct.

Effective
