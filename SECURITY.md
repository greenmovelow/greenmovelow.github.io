# Security Policy

Restoring Democracy's Promise (RDP) takes the security of our website, research infrastructure, sources, readers, and published materials seriously.

We welcome responsible reports from security researchers and members of the public who discover a potential vulnerability affecting RDP-controlled systems.

## Reporting a Security Vulnerability

If you believe you have discovered a security vulnerability involving Restoring Democracy's Promise, please report it privately rather than opening a public GitHub issue.

Email:

**security@restoring-democracy.org**

Please include, when possible:

- A description of the vulnerability.
- The affected page, service, repository, or component.
- Steps necessary to reproduce the issue.
- The potential security impact.
- Any screenshots, logs, or other supporting information that may help us investigate.

Please do not include sensitive personal information unless it is necessary to understand the vulnerability.

## Responsible Disclosure

We ask security researchers to:

- Make a good-faith effort to avoid accessing, modifying, deleting, or retaining data that does not belong to them.
- Avoid actions that could disrupt RDP services or availability.
- Avoid social engineering, phishing, physical intrusion, denial-of-service testing, or attacks against third-party services.
- Stop testing and contact us if you encounter confidential source material, credentials, personal information, or other sensitive data.
- Give us a reasonable opportunity to investigate and address a vulnerability before publicly disclosing technical details.

We will make a good-faith effort to acknowledge legitimate security reports and investigate them as promptly as circumstances permit.

## Scope

This policy applies to systems and repositories operated or controlled by Restoring Democracy's Promise, including:

- `restoring-democracy.org`
- RDP-controlled subdomains
- Public RDP GitHub repositories
- Web applications and interactive exhibits operated by RDP

Third-party platforms and services are governed by their own security policies and vulnerability-reporting procedures.

## Editorial Issues Are Not Security Vulnerabilities

Factual corrections, editorial concerns, requests for clarification, and questions about published reporting should be submitted through RDP's normal editorial contact channels rather than through the security-reporting address.

Journalistic tips or sensitive source communications should use the methods described on RDP's **Secure Tips** page:

https://restoring-democracy.org/secure-tips/

## Privacy

Please do not publicly disclose credentials, authentication information, personal data, confidential source information, or other sensitive material in a GitHub issue, discussion, pull request, or other public forum.

If you encounter such information inadvertently, stop accessing it and report the issue privately.

## Good-Faith Research

RDP supports good-faith security research intended to identify and responsibly report vulnerabilities.

Nothing in this policy authorizes access to systems or data beyond what is necessary to identify and responsibly demonstrate a potential vulnerability, nor does it authorize testing of infrastructure operated by third parties.
