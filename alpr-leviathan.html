Capture

ALPR cameras record every passing vehicle, capturing metadata like the license plate, GPS, timestamp, and an image hash. Flock Safety alone scans approx. 20 billion plates per month.

Vendor Clouds

Data is uploaded to private vendor clouds like Flock and Motorola/Vigilant. Scans from private sources (insurance, repos) are pooled and resold to law enforcement, bypassing direct collection restrictions.

Fusion Layer (Palantir)

Palantir's Gotham platform creates "situational awareness" by integrating ALPR streams with IoT sensors and other datasets, creating a comprehensive "pattern-of-life" index for any vehicle.

Dissemination

The fused intelligence is accessible to local PDs, state fusion centers, and federal agencies like ICE and DHS, often without individualized warrants for broad, speculative searches.

The Architects of the Dragnet

Three key corporate players form the technological backbone of this privatized surveillance system.

Flock Safety

The dominant ALPR vendor, providing cameras and software to over 4,000 cities. Pioneer of "Vehicle Fingerprint™" technology.

Palantir

The critical fusion layer. Its Gotham platform ingests and combines surveillance feeds to create a single, searchable intelligence product.

Motorola/Vigilant

A major competitor with a massive historical database (NVLS) of over 5 billion records, heavily sourced from private industry.

A Constitutional Vacuum

This surveillance network has outpaced legislation and judicial precedent, leaving a significant gap in Fourth Amendment protections.

  • 1

    No Federal Law

    No comprehensive U.S. federal statute governs ALPR use, leading to a patchwork of inconsistent policies.

  • 2

    The Carpenter Precedent

    The landmark Carpenter v. United States ruling, requiring a warrant for cell-site data, has not yet been extended to aggregated ALPR data.

  • 3

    Extra-Judicial Power

    Without reform, agencies using this fused data hold continuous surveillance powers without court review.

A Blueprint for Reform

Addressing the Surveillance Leviathan requires coordinated action.

Enact a Baseline ALPR Privacy Act

Legislation must mandate strict retention limits (e.g., 24-48 hours) for non-hit data and require probable-cause warrants for historical queries.

Mandate Transparency and Oversight

Local governments must hold public hearings before contracting with these vendors and publish data retention and sharing policies.

Press for Disclosure and Litigation

Civil liberties groups should use FOIA to release data-sharing agreements and advance litigation to extend Carpenter warrant protections to ALPR data.
