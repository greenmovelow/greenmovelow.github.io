What the Architecture Does

Captures everything. Every plate. Every vehicle. Every time. Flock's network collects 20 billion reads per month across 49 states — not suspects, everyone.

Routes invisibly. Nlets' pointer index lets any federal agency query any local scan. No audit trail. No notification. The originating department never knows.

Follow One Plate

CAPTURED: FRI 4:47 PM CST — GAS STATION, INTERSTATE 35

01

Camera Capture

T+0 sec

A Flock Safety camera at the gas station photographs your rear plate, captures the image, GPS coordinates, timestamp, and vehicle characteristics. Uploaded to Flock's cloud instantly.

02

Flock National Network

T+1 sec

Your scan is replicated across the national network. 75% of Flock's 4,800 partner agencies participate in the National Lookup Tool — your plate is now searchable by ~7,000 agencies and organizations nationwide. Flock's perpetual license means there is no mechanism to retract this data.

03

Nlets Pointer Index

T+2 sec

Your plate metadata (event number, timestamp, origin agency) is registered in the Nationwide LPR Pointer Index. Any authorized federal agency — CBP, ICE, FBI — can now query this index and be pointed to the originating department. Your local PD has no record this happened.

04

Texas LPR Database

T+minutes

Your data flows into the Texas DPS repository. Stored for a minimum of 3 years. Shared with any authorized criminal justice agency. Reclassified as state intellectual property under SB 1499 — now shielded from public records requests. Your city's 30-day promise is meaningless here.

05

Financial Crimes Intelligence Center

T+hours

The FCIC in Tyler, Texas ingests your plate data and runs it through financial-crime pattern models. Your weekend drive is scored alongside other vehicles' movements. Combinations of time, place, and plate number that look "interesting" are flagged. Your car is now a financial risk asset — not because of anything you did, but because of where you happened to be when a camera blinked.

06

Commercial Data Brokers

T+ongoing

Thomson Reuters CLEAR integrates your plate data with 6+ billion vehicle detections from Vigilant Solutions. LexisNexis Accurint adds it to insurance and fraud investigation databases. Palantir fuses it with banking and property records. Your Friday gas stop is now a data point in a commercial product — sold to banks, insurers, investigators, and federal agencies. Permanently.

End State

You stopped for gas. You committed no crime. Thirty days later, your city deleted the scan. The same record will exist in Texas for three years, in commercial databases indefinitely, and in a federal analyst's pattern model forever.

Tier 3 — The Aggregator

State Repositories & Fusion Centers

TEXAS FCIC / DPS

Operators: Texas DPS LPR Database, Financial Crimes Intelligence Center (FCIC, Tyler TX), state fusion centers (STAC, etc.).

Function: Stores plate data for a minimum of 3 years. Reclassifies it as state intellectual property under HB 3109/SB 1499. Shares with any authorized criminal justice agency — local departments cannot opt out once data enters the system.

The trap: Data from cities with 30-day deletion policies is retained here for years. Texas hosts the largest number of Flock integration nodes in the national network — it functions as America's ALPR laundromat.

Legal Architecture

HB 3109 / SB 1499

Designates collected ALPR data as intellectual property of the FCIC. Shields from FOIA. Requires data transfer to the state when contracts end.

Texas DPS MOU

3-year minimum retention. Shared with any authorized criminal justice agency. No local opt-out.

Tier 2 — The Transport

Nlets — The National Backbone

POINTER INDEX / ROUTING

Operator: National Law Enforcement Telecommunications System (Nlets).

Function: A pointer index — a "card catalog" for plate data. Stores event number, timestamp, and origin agency. Does not store images. Directs queries to the agency that captured the data.

Users: CBP, HSI, ICE, FBI, every state fusion center, and every authorized law enforcement agency.

The trap: Because Nlets only routes queries, no local department can see where its data travels. A department in Iowa uploads a scan; agents in Texas access it days later. Iowa has no record of that transaction. Structural opacity defeats public-records scrutiny.

Built-In Deniability

Each actor claims ignorance:

→

Originating agency: "We deleted it after 30 days."

→

Nlets: "We don't store data — we just route queries."

→

Receiving agency: "We got it through an authorized channel."

No single jurisdiction can produce the full log. The pipeline makes local scans available nationwide while shielding the flow from public view.

Tier 1 — The Sensors

Local ALPR Cameras

FLOCK SAFETY / LOCAL PD

Operators: Flock Safety cameras deployed by 4,800+ local law enforcement agencies across 49 states.

Function: Photographs the rear of every vehicle. Uploads plate number, vehicle image, location, and timestamp to Flock's cloud in real time.

Scale: 20 billion plate reads per month. Every participating department becomes a node in a nationwide network.

The trap: Flock's user agreement grants a perpetual, irrevocable license to customer data. 75% of customers participate in the National Lookup Tool, making their data visible to ~7,000 agencies. There is no mechanism for a city to delete data from the broader network.
