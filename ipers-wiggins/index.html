The Lawsuit Iowa
Still Hasn't Answered in Public

IPERS / WIGGINS V. STATE OF IOWA // WHISTLEBLOWER RETALIATION // POLK COUNTY DISTRICT COURT

The Iowa Public Employees’ Retirement System manages a $40 billion portfolio on behalf of 424,000 teachers, firefighters, corrections officers, and state workers. A newly obtained amended petition — filed January 30, 2026 — alleges that after firing its own risk watchdog, IPERS leadership submitted false information to a state board and later sought executive bonuses.

Editor's note: What follows are allegations contained in a court filing. They are not proven facts.

Core Findings — Amended Petition

Three Allegations the Filing Makes

The former Investment Risk and Operations Officer identified these structural failures before his termination. The petition alleges he raised each with superiors repeatedly — and was fired for it.

01

Flawed Risk Reporting

IPERS did not use standard risk software packages employed by large state pension funds. Instead, the petition alleges, a small Illinois firm used formulas developed by the CIO himself — and staff graphed the output by hand in Microsoft Excel. The petition alleges the risk measure presented to the IPERS board was "far from best practices" and "a misleading measure of risk that was a proxy for tracking error." No external consultant would validate it.

02

An Artificial Benchmark Rubric

The petition alleges the benchmark was structured so that a large portion of the portfolio was benchmarked directly to itself — making underperformance mathematically impossible in those segments. Synthetic exposure via futures was introduced to create easy alpha. "If IPERS' private equity, real estate, direct lending, opportunistic credit and real assets all went to zero," the filing states, "the plan would not underperform its benchmark."

03

False Claims After the Firing

According to the amended petition, after Wiggins was terminated on February 2, 2023, IPERS leadership submitted false information to a state board and later requested bonuses. The filing invokes the Iowa False Claims Act (Iowa Code Ch. 685) and also alleges wrongful discharge in violation of public policy under Iowa Code §97B.7A (2023).

Count I: False Claims Act · Count II: Wrongful Discharge

From the Record

"We don't need a risk officer because we don't have any risk."

— Attributed to CIO Sriram Lakshminarayanan, per petition ¶9 · Characterized as a false statement

This was said shortly after Wiggins was hired, in an attempt to reassign him from the risk officer role. The petition alleges the statement was false: IPERS held more private equity, hedge funds, and leveraged products than a typical pension fund and carried above-average risk relative to peer funds.

"The benchmark return is exactly whatever IPERS' asset classes return."

— Petition ¶20, describing the benchmark structure

The filing characterizes this as "at best a form of engineered outperformance and, at worst, rank intentional misrepresentation." (¶21) According to the petition, a benchmark designed to track the portfolio's own returns cannot, by definition, record underperformance.

After the Firing

According to the petition, IPERS did not replace Wiggins. The CIO — whose conduct Wiggins was tasked with monitoring — assumed de facto authority over risk functions. The petition alleges this is contrary to industry best practices and creates the structural condition under which the false claims were submitted.

Petition ¶25: "After Wiggins was terminated, IPERS did not replace him so currently there is no Investment Risk and Operations Officer."

Documented Sequence

How It Happened

JUNE 2022

Rich Wiggins hired as Investment Risk and Operations Officer

Previously head of risk and strategy at Saudi Aramco. Tasked with monitoring CIO conduct and ensuring IPERS did not "become the next Pennsylvania State Employees Retirement System" — a fund implicated in an FBI investigation over overstated returns.

FALL 2022

Wiggins discovers the risk reporting errors

Raises concerns about risk calculations, benchmark manipulation, and understated fees. Requests formulas and algorithms from the Illinois vendor — denied. Requests outside consultant — ignored.

OCT 2022

Wiggins authors internal memorandum

Recommends bringing in an outside consultant to verify risk calculations. No response received. He continued raising concerns directly with the CIO.

FEB 2, 2023

Wiggins terminated — reason given: "bad fit"

The petition alleges this is pretext for illegal retaliation. Iowa Code §685.3(6) prohibits retaliation against employees who attempt to stop False Claims Act violations.

AFTER TERMINATION

Petition alleges false information submitted; bonuses requested

The amended petition's central new allegation. Specific amounts unknown to Wiggins — IPERS makes bonus decisions in closed session. (¶23A)

JAN 23, 2026

Court grants motion to amend petition

Judge Jason Walke, Fifth Judicial District of Iowa. State of Iowa objected; court granted the amendment over that objection.

JAN 30, 2026

Amended petition filed

E-filed 9:13 AM, Polk County District Court. The amended filing adds more specific allegations, including bonus-related claims and a False Claims Act count.

APR 6, 2026

RDP publishes investigation

First independent press coverage examining the amended allegations in full.

Context: Comparative Risk

Iowa Carries More Risk Than Most

A FY2024 survey by Markov Processes International (MPI) of the 30 largest public pension systems found Iowa and Indiana tied as the most highly leveraged. IPERS holds more private equity, hedge funds, and leveraged products than a typical pension fund — the exact conditions that make accurate risk reporting essential, and the exact conditions the petition alleges were obscured.
