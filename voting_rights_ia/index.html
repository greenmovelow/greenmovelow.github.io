⚙️ Why This Matters

This is not a one-time purge. It converts voter maintenance into a recurring data synchronization exercise with federal systems. Verification shifts from an event-based safeguard to an ongoing data relationship between Iowa election officials and federal databases.

  • The state becomes dependent on federal status codes to maintain its voter rolls
  • Only USCIS or SSA can correct the underlying federal record — local agencies cannot
  • The SAVE algorithm does not eliminate human error — it encodes it
  • Once embedded in code, such systems rarely contract — they scale

Take Action

What You Can Do

The machinery is in motion. Civic vigilance is the only defense.

📞 Contact Legislators

Urge your state legislators to oppose SF 2203 and HF 2501. At publication, each bill had passed only one chamber and neither had become law.

✅ Verify Registration

Periodically check your voter registration — especially if you recently naturalized or changed your name. Visit Vote.gov

🛡️ Bring Documentation

If you are a naturalized citizen, bring your passport, birth certificate, or naturalization papers when you vote.

📬 Respond to Notices

If you receive a notice from your county auditor, respond promptly — the law presumes non-citizenship unless proven otherwise.

Individual vigilance cannot substitute for structural accountability.

Bottom Line

Citizenship Is Not a Database Field

Election law debates often focus on ballots — but this debate is about databases. Iowa's bills would transform voter eligibility into a question of cross-agency identity matching. Infrastructure persists; error rates compound.

The machine is built. It is not yet fully legalized.
The window to unplug it is closing.
